The rain comes down on the tin roof.

It covers the whole roof at once. No gaps. No quiet corners. The tin hums under it, one long sound.

Then a gap opens. A single drop strikes the tin alone. The roar comes back, thinner than before.

The drops come apart. One here. One there. The tin rings in separate places, each ring alone. The air smells of wet earth.

The tin cools under the rain. The sound grows thin, like cloth wearing through. One drop slides down the slope. It hangs at the edge of the roof.

The gutter drips. A drop falls. Nothing. A drop falls. The drip waits longer each time. The water in the gutter thins to a thread. Then to nothing.

One drop. A long quiet. One drop more. The roof is almost still. Above it, the night is wide and dark.

The wet roof holds the first star. A last drop falls into the middle of it. The star breaks. Then it comes back whole.
